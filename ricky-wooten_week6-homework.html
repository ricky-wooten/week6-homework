Authorized versus actual strength

Congress sets a ceiling (authorized strength) each year; services then recruit and retain toward it. This compares the two where both numbers are on file, sorted from furthest under to closest.

Sources and definitions

  • End strength FY1973–FY2021: Office of People Analytics, Population Representation in the Military Services, Table D-39 (DMDC data).
  • End strength FY2022 and FY2024: DoD FY2026 Defense Budget Overview, end-strength table, actuals columns.
  • Authorized strength: Congressional Research Service Insights IN12651 and IN12652, and DoD budget overviews.
  • Recruiting FY2025: Department of War, "FY25 Sees Best Recruiting Numbers in 15 Years" (Dec. 2025); U.S. Coast Guard press release (Nov. 7, 2025).
  • Recruiting FY2023–FY2024: Army.mil and Navy.mil releases; Military.com and National Guard Association reporting.

End strength is the actual headcount on September 30. Authorized strength is the ceiling Congress sets. Sources before FY2022 combine Army and Air Force Guard with their Reserves. Army FY2023 counts accessions; the Army also reported about 55,000 contracts including its delayed-entry pool.
